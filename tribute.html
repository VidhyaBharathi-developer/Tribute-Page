A Life of Inspiration

Dr. Avul Pakir Jainulabdeen Abdul Kalam was one of India's most respected scientists, educators, authors and public figures. He played a major role in India's space and defence programmes and served as the 11th President of India from 2002 to 2007. Known for his humility, discipline, scientific vision and connection with students, he dedicated his life to the development of India and to inspiring young people to dream, learn and contribute to the nation.

Early Life & Education

Dr. A.P.J. Abdul Kalam was born on 15 October 1931 in Rameswaram, Tamil Nadu. He came from a modest family and experienced a simple childhood. His father, Jainulabdeen, was a boat owner and imam, while his mother, Ashiamma, was a homemaker.

As a young student, Kalam developed a strong interest in mathematics and science. His early life taught him the importance of discipline, hard work, self-reliance and perseverance.

He studied physics at Saint Joseph's College, Tiruchirappalli, and later studied aeronautical engineering at the Madras Institute of Technology. His education laid the foundation for his future career in aerospace and defence technology.

His journey from a small town in Tamil Nadu to the highest constitutional office in India became a powerful example of how education, determination and vision can transform a person's life.

Journey Through Time

1931 – Birth

Born on 15 October in Rameswaram, Tamil Nadu.

1954 – Graduation

Completed his physics degree from Saint Joseph's College.

1958 – Aeronautical Engineering

Completed aeronautical engineering at Madras Institute of Technology.

1960 – DRDO

Joined the Aeronautical Development Establishment of India's Defence Research and Development Organisation.

1960s – Space Research

Became associated with India's space research programme and worked on launch vehicle technology.

1980 – SLV-3 Success

Under his leadership, SLV-3 successfully placed the Rohini satellite into near-Earth orbit.

1980s–1990s – Defence Programme

Played a central role in India's Integrated Guided Missile Development Programme.

1997 – Bharat Ratna

Received India's highest civilian award, the Bharat Ratna.

2002 – President of India

Became the 11th President of India.

2007 – Presidency Ends

Completed his five-year term and returned to teaching, writing and public service.

2015 – Final Lecture

Passed away on 27 July 2015 after collapsing while delivering a lecture at IIM Shillong.

Major Contributions

🚀

India's Space Programme

Kalam worked with India's space research programme and contributed to the development of Satellite Launch Vehicle technology. His work supported India's capability to independently launch satellites.

🛰️

SLV-3 Launch Vehicle

He served as Project Director for the SLV-3 programme. The successful 1980 launch placed the Rohini satellite into orbit and represented an important milestone in India's space programme.

🎯

Integrated Guided Missile Development Programme

Kalam was a leading figure in India's missile development programme. The programme contributed to the development of indigenous missile systems including Prithvi, Agni, Akash, Trishul and Nag.

🛡️

Defence Technology

Through his work at DRDO, Kalam contributed to strengthening India's indigenous defence technology capabilities and encouraged technological self-reliance.

🇮🇳

Technology Vision for India

Kalam strongly advocated the use of science and technology for national development. He was associated with Technology Vision 2020, a long-term vision for transforming India into a developed nation.

👨‍🎓

Education and Youth Development

After his scientific and presidential career, Kalam spent considerable time interacting with students and teaching at educational institutions. He encouraged young people to develop ambitious goals and contribute to society.

Scientific Career

DRDO

Kalam joined DRDO in 1960 and worked on aerospace and defence-related projects. His early professional experience introduced him to India's indigenous technology development efforts.

ISRO

He became associated with the Indian Space Research Organisation and served as Project Director of the Satellite Launch Vehicle-3 programme.

SLV-3

The successful 1980 SLV-3 mission made India capable of placing the Rohini satellite into orbit using an Indian launch vehicle.

Defence Research

He returned to DRDO and became a major figure in India's missile development and defence technology programmes.

Scientific Adviser

Kalam served as Scientific Adviser to the Defence Minister and Secretary of the Department of Defence Research and Development.

Principal Scientific Adviser

He later served as Principal Scientific Adviser to the Government of India, contributing to national science and technology policy.
